America's first 250 years were powered by bold, disruptive ideas. What comes next is being built now by the people moving America forward.

PMI U.S. is invested in America — and in the people and projects shaping what's next. To honor America's 250th anniversary and spirit of innovation, PMI U.S. is funding the Community Futures Challenge, a national innovation platform designed in partnership with Arizona State University as official Judging Partner, to uncover America's boldest ideas — and help turn them into reality, anywhere in the country.

Questions, answered.

Can I still apply?

No. Applications for the 2026 Community Futures Challenge closed at 11:59 PM Eastern Time on November 30, 2026.

Who was eligible to apply?

The 2026 nationwide search was open to U.S.-based 501(c)(3) nonprofit organizations in good standing tackling one of America's toughest problems. The lead applicant had to be aged 21 or older, and the project had to serve adults 21 and older within a defined U.S. community. Early-stage, pilot, and scaling projects qualified; fully self-sustaining initiatives did not.

What communities were eligible?

Applications were open to projects serving defined geographic communities in the United States, across all 50 states and Puerto Rico. Applicants did not need to be located in, or serve, one of the Innovation Tour cities. Eligible projects were asked to demonstrate a clear connection to a specific U.S. community and show how the proposed solution addresses a local need with potential relevance beyond that community.

Could an organization apply through a fiscal sponsor?

Yes. The fiscal sponsor had to be a 501(c)(3) in good standing and apply under its own name and EIN; it also receives the grant. The sponsored group can lead the project work.

Could organizations that serve people under 21 apply?

Yes. The activities paid for with the grant must serve adults 21 and older, but an organization can serve other ages in its other programs.

What kinds of projects were we looking for?

You name it. Workforce development. Economic mobility. Public infrastructure. Civic technology. Entrepreneurship. Anything that materially strengthens a U.S. community. We favor the specific, the replicable, and the measurable over the broadly aspirational.

When did submissions close, and what happens next?

Submissions opened September 1, 2026 and closed at 11:59 PM Eastern Time on November 30, 2026. Finalists will be notified in January 2027. The five winners are announced and recognized at a dedicated event in early 2027.

How are proposals judged?

An independent panel of nationally acclaimed judges scores entries across five dimensions, each on a 0–10 scale weighted by importance: community impact (25%), feasibility (20%), innovation (20%), sustainability (20%), and founder/team (15%) — 100% in total. The full panel is published on this site under Judges.

What role does PMI U.S. have in selecting winners?

Submissions are evaluated by an independent panel of judges using the published selection criteria. PMI U.S. is supporting the program and funding the awards, while the judging process is designed to ensure credibility, transparency, and alignment with the Challenge's stated goals.

Are there restrictions on how the $50,000 can be used?

The grant is unrestricted, but recipients are expected to deliver against the project plan submitted in their application and to participate in light reporting and storytelling moments tied to the program. Capital, construction and equipment costs are fine, as long as they are part of that project plan.

Will applicant information or stories be used publicly?

PMI U.S. and ASU may highlight selected applicants, finalists, and winners through program communications, storytelling, events, or media opportunities. Any public use of participant names, project details, images, or stories should be handled in accordance with program terms and any required permissions or consents.
